Elizabeth was of Northampton, Massachusetts, and married William Allis of Hatfield in 1703 or 1704. She bore five children: Mary in 1705, Lois in 1707/8 and Eliphalet in 1710 at Hatfield, and then, after the family moved upriver to Sunderland, Zebediah in 1713 and Elizabeth in 1716.

In later life she and her husband moved again, across the river to Montague, and there she died on 1 May 1758. The town set it down in a single line: “Allis. May first 1758, Elizabeth Allis wife to Mr. William Allis, died in Montague.” Her husband outlived her by five years. Her maiden name was Davis, of Northampton — though that comes down on the family’s word rather than from anything written in her own girlhood, and the Montague book that records her death calls her only “Elizabeth Allis wife to Mr. William Allis.”

Michael Mitchell (Wallingford Conn. → Deerfield)

Ahnentafel #1540 · his mother’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s mother’s father’s father (10 generations back) · Documented — person and parent-link established

Michael Mitchell was a Wallingford, Connecticut man — a proprietor of the town, casting lots with the other townsmen in 1695–6 and still there in 1706. Where he came from before that nobody has ever been able to say. About 1694 he married Sarah, daughter of John Catlin of Deerfield.

He moved his family to Deerfield, the most exposed town on the Massachusetts frontier. On the morning of 29 February 1704, when the French and their allies came over the stockade, his whole family escaped unharmed — which in that town, on that morning, was the exception. His wife’s father and brother were burned in their own house a few hundred yards away.

His children were William, born 1697; Michael, born 8 March 1700/1, afterwards a soldier in Father Rasle’s war and later of Wethersfield, Connecticut; Joseph, born 1705, the weaver, through whom this family descends; and Samuel, born 7 March 1712, very probably the Samuel Mitchell granted land at Sunderland in 1737 on condition that he stayed five years. Sarah died on 30 December 1736.

His Wallingford years matter more than they look. The Tuttles were farming west of the river at Wallingford in exactly those years, and Gershom Tuttle was born there in 1714 — so when Michael’s granddaughter Tabitha married Gershom’s son at Deerfield in 1757, it was not a chance meeting a hundred miles from home. It was two Wallingford families who had both gone north, marrying each other.

Sarah Catlin (m. ~1694 – d. 1736)

Ahnentafel #1541 · his mother’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s mother’s father’s mother (10 generations back) · Documented — person and parent-link established

Sarah Catlin was a daughter of John Catlin — a Wethersfield man who taught school at Newark in New Jersey, served as that town’s attorney and five years its selectman, and then brought his family to Deerfield about 1683, where he was dignified with the title of “Mr.” — and of his wife Mary Baldwin. About 1694 she married Michael Mitchell, and her eldest child was born in 1697.

That marriage is probably why she lived. On 29 February 1704, when the raid came, she was under her own husband’s roof, and the Mitchell household came through untouched. Her father’s did not. His buildings were all burned and he and his son Jonathan were consumed with them; another son was killed in the Meadow Fight later the same morning; a son and three daughters were carried off toward Canada. Two of those daughters were her sisters — Elizabeth, wife of James Corse, and Mary, wife of Thomas Wells — and both were killed on the march. In a town where a hundred and twelve people were marched away in a single morning, it was said of her father that “no family suffered more than his.”

She bore at least four sons — William, Michael, Joseph and Samuel — and died on 30 December 1736, about two months before her granddaughter Tabitha was born. She lies in the Old Burying Ground at Deerfield, in the same ground as the common grave of the dead of 1704, her father and her brother among them; time and weather have taken the place of her own grave.

Simon Tuttle (1647 – 1719 Wallingford Conn.)

Ahnentafel #3072 · his mother’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father (11 generations back) · Probable — the IDENTIFICATION or the LINK rests on one source, or is in conflict

Simon Tuttle was baptised at New Haven on 28 March 1647, reckoned the ninth child of William Tuttle, the immigrant, and his wife Elizabeth, on good grounds that no record yet read has proved. He was bred a cooper. When Wallingford was laid out in 1670 he put his name on the proprietors’ list and was assigned lot No. 13 with eight acres of out-land, but he never took the allotment up; he stayed on at New Haven, where he was still drawing his share in the third division in 1680. That list credits him with “two heads” — a wife, and no children yet — so he had married Abigail about 1679 or 1680. He was of New Haven through 1683 and of Wallingford from 1685, moving, it seems, after his mother died in December 1684.

At Wallingford he did well. He drew lot No. 52 with a hundred and four acres in 1686 and lot No. 14 in 1690, was rated at £79 on the grand list of 1701, and from 1708–9 served the town as gauger and packer, the officer who inspects goods sold by weight and measure. He spent thirty years selling off what his father had left at New Haven, and the family genealogy of 1883 quotes him naming him in those papers: in 1677–8, land sold to a cordwainer that “my father, Mr. William Tuttle, bought of John Punderson”; in 1686, six acres “formerly belonging to my father, Mr. William Tuttle”; in 1705, to his cousin Joshua, all his right in the Branford division “set to my father, Mr. William Tuttle.” In 1673 he and five of his brothers signed a right-of-way agreement; the paper, with his signature on it, was still in existence two hundred years later.

He had dealings at Branford too. On 5 August 1685 the town meeting there gave Christopher Tod liberty to sell his house and land to Simon Tuttle, and on 5 November 1687, styled “Simon Tuttle of Wallingford … Cooper,” he sold Richard Rosewell of New Haven a Branford house with a three-acre home lot, twenty-three acres adjoining, half of Wheadon’s Swamp and eighteen acres of farm land yet to be laid out, for thirty-eight pounds.

He gave land to his sons in his own lifetime, as his son Timothy would in turn: nine and a half acres to Timothy on 10 July 1711, land “I bought of Roger Tyler,” and a fourteen-acre house lot to Daniel on 23 June 1715. Simon Tuttle died on 16 April 1719 at Wallingford, and is said to lie in the Center Street burying ground there. His widow Abigail outlived him by three years and died on 11 August 1722.

John Doolittle (1655 New Haven – aft. 1717 Conn.)

Ahnentafel #3074 · his mother’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s mother’s father (11 generations back) · Documented — person and parent-link established

John Doolittle was born at New Haven on 14 June 1655, a son of Abraham Doolittle — who had come to New Haven from Boston in 1644, took the freeman’s oath there, served as the county’s chief executive officer, and was one of the founders of Wallingford. His mother was Abraham’s first wife, Joan Alling: no record of the time names her as such, but her father’s will of January 1657 left ten pounds to “her children”, and John and his three older siblings were then Abraham’s only children. His father is not in doubt: in 1681 his childless uncle John Doolittle of Rumney Marsh, near Boston, left fifty pounds “unto John Dowlettell the Son of my Brother Abraham” and a piece of Lynn saltmarsh to Abraham for life and then “to his son John for ever”; in 1687 father and son sold that marsh together, signing as “Abraham Doolittle & John Doolittle, father and Son”; and in 1690 Abraham’s will gave “my son John” ten acres at Wharton’s Brook. On 13 February 1682/3 John married Mary Peck, and settled at Wallingford, the town his father had helped to lay out.

Their children included Esther, born 24 January 1683, who also married a Tuttle; Samuel in 1685; Thankful, who the family’s genealogists say was born in April 1688 (the town’s copied register does not have her birth), and who married Timothy Tuttle in 1706; and Benjamin, born 1695, who took his degree at Yale in 1716, married Lydia Todd the next year, and was settled as the minister of Northfield, up on the Massachusetts frontier, from 1718 until he died suddenly in January 1748. It was to that uncle’s house that Thankful’s son Gershom went when he first travelled north as a young man — which is how a Wallingford family came to have a foothold a hundred miles up the Connecticut River.

Mary died on 1 September 1710. John married again on 29 January 1717, to Grace Blakeslee, and after that he disappears from view; he was sixty-one then, and the year of his death is not known.

Samuel Allis (1647 Braintree – 1691 Hatfield)

Ahnentafel #3076 · his mother’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s mother’s father’s father (11 generations back) · Documented — person and parent-link established

Samuel Allis was born at Braintree, Massachusetts, on 24 February 1647, the son of William Allis and Mary his wife. Braintree wrote him down under the year 1646, the year then beginning in March and February being counted the twelfth month: “Samuel, sonne of William Alyes & Mary his wife was Borne the 24th. of ye 12 month.” His father was a Braintree freeman of 1640 who removed about 1661 up the Connecticut River to the new town of Hatfield.

Samuel followed him there and took his own home lot as a young man of twenty-two. On 4 February 1669 the town granted him a house lot sixteen rods wide next to Samuel Marsh’s, with land in the Plain above the great meadow and ten acres in the swamp by the Mill river — “provided he build a sufficient dwelling house upon his lot by or before the last of May in the year 1671, otherwise to forfeit his land.” Daniel Belden had the lot on the other side. He built, and he stayed: a carpenter and builder, living on the east side of Hatfield street.

About 1675–6 he married a woman named Alice, whose surname nobody ever wrote down. Seven children followed: Mehitable on 2 July 1677; Samuel on 20 February 1679; William on 19 October 1680, from whom this family descends; Mary on 6 July 1682; Thomas on 12 March 1684; Sarah about 1685; and Rebecca on 29 November 1687.

Samuel Allis died at Hatfield on 9 March 1691, aged forty-four, leaving Alice with seven children all under fifteen. She remarried in 1696 and was killed in the raid on Deerfield of 29 February 1704; their son Samuel was killed in the same attack, and their daughter Mary was carried to Canada and lived to come home.
