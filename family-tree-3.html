John Bell was born about 1807 in South Carolina and had moved to Barbour County, Alabama before 1837, where all of his children were born. The 1850 census finds him at forty-three, farming, with his wife Laney — also South Carolina born — and five children at home.

Two of those children matter to this family: William, aged eleven, who would die a Confederate pensioner in 1924 leaving an estate that scattered across his nieces and nephews; and John, aged nine, who would become John T. Bell and father Thomas Jefferson Bell.

His son’s obituary says he came to Alabama from Darlington, South Carolina, about 1830, and settled in the neighbourhood where the family stayed for the next century. He is the point at which this line leaves Alabama — and Darlington District is where everything before him has to be looked for.
