John Wesley Truett (20 Oct 1833 – 17 Apr 1914)

Ahnentafel #88 · his father’s mother’s mother’s father’s father’s father (6 generations back) · Documented — person and parent-link established

John Wesley Truett was born on 20 October 1833 and died on 17 April 1914. He served as a private in Company D of the 32nd Alabama Infantry during the Civil War, enlisting on 15 October 1862 at Coffeeville, and was hospitalized within weeks with chronic rheumatism that kept him listed as absent for the rest of the war. A voucher he signed himself at Dalton, Georgia, on 30 May 1863 is the oldest surviving signature of a direct ancestor in this family's records, and he was later enumerated, at age 73, in the 1907 Alabama Census of Confederate Veterans.

Evidence

Page 11 is a voucher he signed himself at Dalton on 30 May 1863: the oldest signature of a direct ancestor recovered in this project. Colonel Gershom Tuttle III (11 Jun 1769 – 5 Sep 1823)

Ahnentafel #192 · his mother’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father (7 generations back) · Documented — person and parent-link established

Colonel Gershom Tuttle III was born on 11 June 1769 and died on 5 September 1823. According to the 1883 Tuttle genealogy, he moved from Bristol, Connecticut, to Paris in Oneida County, New York, about 1790, and then around 1800 to Jefferson County, New York, where he opened a 275-acre farm near what became Watertown. He was one of the town's first highway commissioners, helped build the first grist mill in the town of Rutland in 1806, and opened a tavern on the main highway to the lake country that became famous during the War of 1812, in which he served as Lieutenant Colonel of the 76th New York Militia, a regiment that bore his name.

He married Pamelia Strong Clark on 8 July 1788 in Farmington, Connecticut, and the family later removed to Vigo County, Indiana, in 1816, where he served as county commissioner from 1820. He was described by his contemporaries as a man of excellent character and genial temper, fond of a joke and a capital storyteller, and he is buried at Otter Creek Union Cemetery, North Terre Haute, in Vigo County, Indiana.
