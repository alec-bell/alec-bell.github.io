John Wells (bur. 1618 Ringstead, Northants)

Ahnentafel #24578 · his mother’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s mother’s father (14 generations back) · Hypothesis — a candidate, not tied in

John Wells of Ringstead was a freeholder there in 1605, listed as “John Weales” beside “Symon Tootwell,” and his will of 1618 survives, though it has not yet been read. By the account of a genealogy of 1952, he was the father of Isabel, who married Symon Tootell, and when he made his will in 1618 he remembered her, his son-in-law, and all four of his Tuttell grandsons. If that is right, the will is the earliest paper to name William Tuttle, then about ten years old.

Evidence

Full dates: buried at Ringstead 18 March 1617/8; will dated 20 Jan 1617/8, proved 25 Mar 1618 (Archdeaconry of Northampton, M.211, unread)

Also recorded: of Ringstead, co. Northampton; will 1618

Per Jacobus: his will mentions his daughter Isabel and Symon as son-in-law, and names all four of his Tuttell grandsons.

Outstanding: his 1618 will, unread and not yet located; no Wells of Ringstead appears in the OCR of the 1888 Archdeaconry calendar, which is poor, so that is not a negative. It would also test nodes 12288 and 12289 and William’s parentage. Lead only, not adopted: a compiled page says Symon’s will mentions twenty pounds given to William by his grandfather John Welles. ADD (6 Nov 2026): ⭐ His wife, read at the ink: “Hellene well wyfe of John well was buriede this xxijth of Maye” 1612, Ringstead register [TUTTLE-271]. A Hellen Well married Hierome Lynsey at Ringstead on 20 Oct 1619 — possibly his daughter. No second John Wells appears in the Ringstead register read 1569–1661 (with a 1596–1606 lapse and some pages unread). Nothing yet ties him to Isabel: the 1618 will is still the test, and it can now be asked whether it names a wife other than Hellene.

⭐ 2 Nov 2026 [TUTTLE-256]: will located: Calendar of Wills, Archdeacon of Northampton (1888), p. 152: “Wells… John: Ringstead M. 211,” 1618 (second series). Freeholder of Ringstead, 1605, “John Weales.” Also in the Montagu Musters Book 1602–23 as Wells, John, of Ringstead (lane’s reading). These document the man; only the will can tie him to Isabel. Correction: the “not yet located” and OCR caveats in the Outstanding above are superseded.

⭐ 5 Nov 2026 — his burial read at the image [TUTTLE-265]. Ringstead register (NRO 280p/1; Ancestry 9198, set 101392473, img 44), under 1617: “John wells was buryed the 18th of March” — seven days before his will was proved. The first record of him read at the source. It names no kin, so it does not touch his link to Isabel; the grade stays hyp.

Thomas Totehyll (~1506 – aft. 1552 Woodford)

Ahnentafel #49152 · his mother’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father (15 generations back) · Hypothesis — a candidate, not tied in

Thomas Totehyll is the farthest point anyone has claimed for this family, and the claim is a guess: the genealogy of 1952 that names him says only that he was “probably” Richard Tootill’s father, and no record of him has yet been read for this tree. It places him at Woodford in Northamptonshire, born about 1506 under Henry VII, assessed for the subsidy there on 10 November 1544, and called on again and again to witness his neighbours’ wills, from Robert Crosse’s in 1524 to Elizabeth Whitbred’s in 1552.

⚠ The weakest link on this chart: Jacobus himself says only “Probably father of” Richard, on shared residence. No will or record is known to connect them. He witnessed the wills of Robert Crosse 1524, Robert Pashler 1538, Sir William Longe 1541, Richard Lyncoln 1545 and Elizabeth Whitbred 1552, which, with a birth about 1506, would make him a witness at about eighteen.
