Pamelia Strong Clark (b. 4 Nov 1770 Farmington, Conn. – 15 Apr 1851 Nashville, Ind.)

Ahnentafel #193 · his mother’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s mother (7 generations back) · Documented — person and parent-link established

Pamelia Strong Clark was born on 4 November 1770 in Farmington, Connecticut, and died on 15 April 1851, by one account, or 15 November 1851 by another — sources disagree on the exact date — near Nashville, Indiana. She married Gershom Tuttle on 8 July 1788 in Farmington; according to the 1883 Tuttle genealogy, her brother was a United States Army officer who died while commanding the U.S. arsenal at Troy, New York, and her nephew was General Newman Strong Clark of the Department of the Pacific.

She outlived her husband by twenty-eight years, following one of their children south, and is buried at Southview Cemetery in Nashville, Brown County, Indiana, ninety miles from where he lies. Her middle name, Strong, was carried on through her descendants for four generations, though a search of the standard genealogy of the Strong family has so far failed to confirm any direct Strong ancestry for her.

John Roush was born on 22 November 1785 and died on 21 January 1861 in Meigs County, Ohio. They are buried in a family plot beside Regina's father, Jonas, though their graves are unmarked. Their son Benjamin's place in the family is recorded in a 1928 genealogy, though this last link has not yet been confirmed by an original record.

Gershom Tuttle II, remembered as "the Patriot," was born 22 August 1738 in Farmington, Connecticut (in the New Cambridge parish, now Bristol), and died 5 January 1818. During the Revolutionary War he served as a teamster, conducting wagon trains of army provisions — an operation that hauled some 3,000 barrels of supplies, roughly 500 tons, from Connecticut into New York State. He married Tabitha Mitchell at Deerfield, Massachusetts, on 22 December 1757. The couple later moved first to New Hampshire, then to Clinton in Oneida County, New York, and finally to Watertown, New York, where Gershom served as the first deacon of the town's first Congregational church, organized in 1803.
