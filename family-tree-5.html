Moore Stephens (b. ~1796 NC – Sumter Co. AL 1850)

Ahnentafel #160 · his father’s mother’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father (7 generations back) · Documented — person and parent-link established

Moore Stephens was born about 1796 in North Carolina and was still living in Sumter County, Alabama, in 1850, later moving on to Newton County, Mississippi. He married Rebecca Moize on 3 September 1817 in Orange County, North Carolina, and the two were still together, and still recorded as North Carolina-born, in both the 1850 and 1860 census. He was one of ten heirs at law of Mary Thorn's 1846 Greene County, Alabama estate, alongside nine named siblings — William, Lewis, Charles, and James Stephens, and sisters Nancy Barnett, Mary Barnett, Lucinda Ball, Elizabeth Beavers, and Esther Hitt.

After his sister Esther's death, he and Rebecca raised her two orphaned children alongside their own, naming a daughter Easter in her memory. The 1850 slave schedule for Sumter County records that Moore held one enslaved person, an unnamed Black girl about seven years old.

Colonel Gershom Tuttle III (1769 – 1823)

Ahnentafel #192 · his mother’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father (7 generations back) · Documented — person and parent-link established

Colonel Gershom Tuttle III was born on 11 June 1769 and died on 5 September 1823. According to the 1883 Tuttle genealogy, he moved from Bristol, Connecticut, to Paris in Oneida County, New York, about 1790, and then around 1800 to Jefferson County, New York, where he opened a 275-acre farm near what became Watertown. He was one of the town's first highway commissioners, helped build the first grist mill in the town of Rutland in 1806, and opened a tavern on the main highway to the lake country that became famous during the War of 1812, in which he served as Lieutenant Colonel of the 76th New York Militia, a regiment that bore his name.

He married Pamelia Strong Clark on 8 July 1788 in Farmington, Connecticut, and the family later removed to Vigo County, Indiana, in 1816, where he served as county commissioner from 1820. He was described by his contemporaries as a man of excellent character and genial temper, fond of a joke and a capital storyteller, and he is buried at Otter Creek Union Cemetery, North Terre Haute, in Vigo County, Indiana.

Pamelia Strong Clark (1770 Farmington – 1851)

Ahnentafel #193 · his mother’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s mother (7 generations back) · Documented — person and parent-link established

Pamelia Strong Clark was born on 4 November 1770 in Farmington, Connecticut, and died on 15 April 1851, by one account, or 15 November 1851 by another — sources disagree on the exact date — near Nashville, Indiana. She married Gershom Tuttle on 8 July 1788 in Farmington; according to the 1883 Tuttle genealogy, her brother was a United States Army officer who died while commanding the U.S. arsenal at Troy, New York, and her nephew was General Newman Strong Clark of the Department of the Pacific.

She outlived her husband by twenty-eight years, following one of their children south, and is buried at Southview Cemetery in Nashville, Brown County, Indiana, ninety miles from where he lies. Her middle name, Strong, was carried on through her descendants for four generations, though a search of the standard genealogy of the Strong family has so far failed to confirm any direct Strong ancestry for her.

John Roush was born on 22 November 1785 and died on 21 January 1861 in Meigs County, Ohio. He was the son of George Roush and Catharine Zerkel, and on 18 May 1808 he married his own cousin, Regina Roush, with Justice of the Peace Francis Watkins presiding. The couple settled in the Nease Settlement of Sutton Township, Meigs County, Ohio, moving there from Graham Station, Mason County, Virginia, sometime between the births of their children Benjamin, born in Virginia in August 1816, and Hannah, born in the Nease Settlement in May 1820.

They are buried in a family plot beside Regina's father, Jonas, though their graves are unmarked. Their son Benjamin's place in the family is recorded in a 1928 genealogy, though this last link has not yet been confirmed by an original record.
