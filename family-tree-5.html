Francis R. Williams lived on the Mississippi side of the Alabama line, in the strip of pine country where Greene and Clarke Counties meet. The family’s account gives his name in full as Francis Roundtree Williams, born in February 1840 and dead in April 1907. He married Mary J. Lewis, and they raised a large family in that border country. William Curtis Truett (1867 – 1925 New Prospect AL)

Ahnentafel #44 · his father’s mother’s mother’s father’s father (5 generations back) · Documented — person and parent-link established

William Curtis Truett was born on 25 July 1867, the son of John Wesley Truett and Emily Caroline Ward, and grew up at Chilton in Clarke County, Alabama, in a house that held three generations at once: his father, a Confederate veteran who could not write his own name; his mother; his brothers and his sister; and his grandmother Alcey, the widow of the preacher William Hampton Truett, who was sixty-seven in 1880 and living under her son’s roof.

On 17 June 1886, not yet nineteen, he married Amanda Webb in Clarke County. They farmed at Clarksville, and by the summer of 1900 there were six children in the house — Tela, Jane, Bigy, Lillie, Tamie and the baby Bessie. Bessie and Jane were still living in 1960, when his second wife’s obituary named them as her step-daughters — Mrs. Bessie Shewmake of Satsuma and Mrs. Jane Hutchinson of Juniper, Georgia. He and Amanda could both read and write, which neither his father nor his grandmother could do: the first literate generation on this line. Thomas William, the “Tamie” of that household, was Alec’s great-grandfather.

Amanda was alive in that house in June 1900 and dead or gone not long after, and he married a second time, to Zella Shewmake, born 1 June 1878.

He farmed at New Prospect, a settlement whose little Baptist church had been constituted about 1860 by an Elder C. J. Miles, who for his trouble was paid “one dollar per day while in active service.” When the county’s thirty-four Baptist churches were apportioned in 1892, New Prospect was asked for sixteen dollars — near the smallest sum in the county. All the places of his life sat within a few miles of one another: Chilton, where he grew up; Clarksville, where he farmed in middle life; and New Prospect, with Harris Creek running between them.

Two Truett brothers married two Webb sisters — he took Amanda, and his younger brother John Wesley took Valley. That brother died at his home in Coffeeville on a Wednesday morning in February 1936, of pneumonia after a few days’ illness, and was buried at Prospect church, the Rev. T. C. Paul of Grove Hill officiating. By then the family had scattered: a brother George and a sister, Sarah Giger, were both in Florida, where their mother had gone as an old woman.

He died at New Prospect on 21 December 1925, aged fifty-eight, and is buried in the church graveyard under a low double marker of pale granite, TRUETT across the top, his own name on the right and Zella’s on the left. She outlived him by thirty-five years, and the stone was almost certainly cut when she joined him in 1960. Amanda, the mother of his eldest son, is not on it, and is not buried there.

Amanda Webb (1868 – aft. 1900 Clarke Co.)

Ahnentafel #45 · his father’s mother’s mother’s father’s mother (5 generations back) · Documented — person and parent-link established

Amanda Webb was born in July 1868 in Alabama and married William Curtis Truett in Clarke County on 17 June 1886, when she was not quite eighteen and he not quite nineteen.

They farmed at Clarksville. By the summer of 1900 she was thirty-one and fourteen years married, and had borne six children, all six living and all six in the house with her: Tela, thirteen; Jane, twelve; Bigy, ten; Lillie, eight; Tamie, six; and Bessie, one. The Webbs were old Clarke County people. A William Webb came into the county about 1812 and died at his home there in February 1853 at about seventy-eight; the Webb mill on the Tallahatta was built about 1836; and Webbs were among the families forted up at McGrew’s Fort in the Indian troubles of 1810. Whether Amanda belonged to them is not known.

Benjamin Nott (~1817 OH – 1891 Marietta)

Ahnentafel #52 · his mother’s father’s mother’s father’s father (5 generations back) · Probable — the IDENTIFICATION or the LINK rests on one source, or is in conflict

Benjamin Nott — the name was written Knott about as often as Nott — was born in Ohio about 1815 to 1817 and farmed in Adams Township, near Lowell, in Washington County. His mother was Polly (Roach) Nott. His father was very probably an elder Benjamin Nott “of Adams” died insolvent in 1822, leaving a widow Polly; he was one of several Nott householders in that corner of Ohio, and if he was the father, as seems likely, the boy was orphaned as a small boy on a broke farm on Calls Creek.

On the evening of Sunday 13 March 1836, in Morgan County, up the Muskingum, he married Margaret Kirk; their son John W. swore to her maiden name sixty-four years later. By 1850 they were farming in Adams Township, both thirty-three, with five children — Henrietta, Lucina, Frances, Pearly and William — and a Virginia-born woman of sixty-eight, Polly Morris, living with them — Benjamin’s mother, remarried since his father’s death. A year later she deeded him her fifty acres and took them back on a lease for her life; in 1861, “aged and infirm,” she left “my son Benjamin Nott” her share of her own father’s estate. In 1860 the household stood at Lowell: Benjamin forty-three, a farmer; Margaret forty-three; P. J. fifteen; William F. thirteen; and Jos M eleven. That eleven-year-old is believed to be Joseph McGee Knott, who named Benjamin and Margaret as his parents and Lowell as his birthplace at the end of his own life; the ages do not quite line up, so the link is probable rather than certain.

In July 1861, at forty-six, he volunteered for Company A of the Thirty-sixth Ohio Infantry — enlisted 29 July 1861, a private, discharged 4 September 1864. By 1870 the family had moved to Marietta, where the household was Benjamin fifty-three, Margaret fifty-two, John W. twenty-three, Sarah twenty, a baby Henry, and three boarders.

His last years were hard. In December 1890 the probate court recorded “P. J. Nott, guardian of Benjamin Nott, an Insane person” filing an inventory — the guardian was his own child, the P. J. of the 1860 household. He died at Marietta on 6 May 1891 of la grippe, the influenza that went round the world between 1889 and 1891, and the estate was settled that July: charged with nothing at all, credited with $10.91, and that sum found due the child who had cared for him. He lies in Oak Grove Cemetery at Marietta, lot 90, block 19, under an upright stone.
