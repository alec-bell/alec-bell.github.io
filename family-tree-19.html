Joseph Lamb (~1782 NC – 1855 Wayne Co. IN)

Ahnentafel #216 · his mother’s father’s mother’s mother’s father’s father’s father (7 generations back) · Documented — person and parent-link established

Joseph Lamb was born in North Carolina about 1782. He married Lydia Adamson there in November 1806, her brother Samuel standing bondsman — the clerk wrote her name “Lidya Addison” — and they were members of the Society of Friends. Nine children were born to them, of whom seven grew up: Ezekiel, Esther, Smith, Elias, Mournen, Joseph and Ithamar. The family was living in Randolph County, about sixteen miles north of Asheboro, in 1814, when his son Elias was born there.

In April 1829 he took the family out of North Carolina for Indiana — the ordinary Quaker departure from the slaveholding South — and settled in Clay Township, Wayne County, on land adjoining Perry Township that a widow, Eve Shoemaker, had first taken up in 1821. He farmed there for the rest of his life, and his sons and his wife’s brother settled around him: when the Clay Township school section was sold in 1832 the buyers included Samuel Adamson, and his son Ithamar was still living on that land forty years later.

Lydia died first — in 1844, his son Elias remembered, though the year is also given as 1837. Joseph died on 29 July 1855, aged seventy-three, and lies in Sugar Grove Cemetery in Wayne County under a small marble stone, weathered but still readable: JOSEPH LAMB DIED 29th Day of the July of year 1855.

John Bish lived in Fairfield County, Ohio, about eighteen miles east of Columbus, where his daughter Susannah was born in November 1818. He may be the John Bish who married an Anne Campbell in Rockingham County, Virginia, in November 1810. He and his wife Ann Elizabeth raised ten children to maturity.

In 1836 he brought the family to Wayne County, Indiana, and settled in Clay Township. The Bishes lived in Wayne County many years. Later they removed to Miami County, Indiana, where his wife died; afterwards he came back to Wayne County and died on the old homestead, on 20 August 1866. He lies in Sugar Grove Cemetery, Row 9 of the north section — the same burying ground as Joseph Lamb — under a tall marble marker now so badly spalled that the name is only a shadow, though “AUG. 20, 1866” and “AGED” can still be made out.

Emanuel Nease (~1792 Va. – 1853/54 Mason Co.)

Ahnentafel #248 · his mother’s mother’s mother’s mother’s father’s father’s father (7 generations back) · Documented — person and parent-link established

Emanuel Nease was born in Virginia about 1792, a son of Henry Nease, who on one spring day in 1817 gave him a hundred-acre farm in Mason County “for the natural love and affection which I bear” him and gave his brother Henry the farm next to it. Emanuel farmed in Mason County, on the Virginia bank of the Ohio, with his wife Katharine. In 1850 he was fifty-eight and she fifty-three, living next door to the Roushes. On 4 October 1853 he signed his will in his own hand. Every one of his sons, it says, had been given a horse, four sheep and two hogs on reaching twenty-one, and he left the same to his youngest, Levi, so that he would not be short; everything else went to Katharine, and after her to all his children, “sons and daughters Share and Share alike without any differance whatsoever.” He was dead by July 1854. In March 1865, a month before the war ended, his children met to sign over the home farm to the youngest, Levi, keeping their mother’s right to live on it for the rest of her life. George Nease signed among them, “as Heirs at law of Emanuel Nease deceased”. That deed settles whose son George was, though George’s own death record, filled in thirty-four years later, would call his father Samuel.
