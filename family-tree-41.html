Symon Tootell (d. 1630 Ringstead, Northants)

Ahnentafel #12288 · his mother’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father (13 generations back) · Hypothesis — a candidate, not tied in

Whether Symon Tootell was William Tuttle’s father is not yet proved, but the records make him the likeliest man. He lived at Ringstead, a village on the Nene in Northamptonshire, and he stood among its leading parishioners: in 1622 he signed the parish register as churchwarden, as “Symon Tootehill,” beneath the vicar James Southwell. He was already a man of property there in 1605, when the return of the county’s freeholders listed seven at Ringstead, among them “Symon Tootwell” and “John Weales.” On 26 December 1607 a William, son of Symon, was christened in the parish.

He was buried at Ringstead on 15 June 1630; the clerk first wrote the fourteenth, struck it out and wrote the fifteenth. He left a will, entered that year in the Archdeacon of Northampton’s court, and it still lies unread in book O at folio 212. Within five years the Tootells had gone. William and Richard Tuttell of Ringstead sailed on the Planter in April 1635 with their households, and with them went an Isbell Tuttell, aged seventy.

James Allen (bap. 1586 Kempston – 1657 Kempston)

Ahnentafel #12298 · his mother’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s mother’s father’s mother’s father (13 generations back) · Documented — person and parent-link established

James Allen was a blacksmith at Kempston, a parish of scattered hamlets just south-west of Bedford, where he was baptised in 1586 and where he raised his family. Two of his children crossed the Atlantic: his son Roger, who became treasurer of the New Haven Colony, and his daughter Joan, the wife of Abraham Doolittle. When he made his will in January 1657 he remembered them both by name as “living now in New England”, leaving each ten pounds and ten more to be shared among their children. He kept a feather bed and bolster back for Joan: they would go to her sister Martha “in case my daughter Joane comes not to demand the same within twoo yeares. If she doth then I will both the featherbedd and feather bolster shalbe hers.” The rest went to the children who had stayed: William, Martha (Mrs. Richard Parkes), the children of Mary Warren, and his son John, who inherited the house at Kempston Wood End and the close called Nine Leyes. He was buried at Kempston on 29 December 1657. His son William and William's daughter Elizabeth followed him to the churchyard within weeks, and Roger sailed home from New Haven to settle the estate, back again by August 1658.
