Dea. Timothy Tuttle (~1682 New Haven – 1756 Wallingford)

Ahnentafel #1536 · his mother’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father (10 generations back) · Documented — person and parent-link established

Deacon Timothy Tuttle was born about 1682 in New Haven, Connecticut, and moved with his family to Wallingford as a small child. He later cleared land with his own hands a mile east of the center of what became Cheshire, Connecticut, and built a house there said to be among the town's earliest frame houses. He married Thankful Doolittle on 2 November 1706 at Wallingford; after her death he remarried three more times, the last time in 1749, and he died in 1756.

A respected figure in his community, he served as sergeant, church moderator and deacon. In 1736 he deeded his own share of land in a Massachusetts frontier township to his son Gershom, giving it, as the deed itself records, out of "love, good will and fatherly affection."

Simon Tuttle was baptized in New Haven on 28 March 1647, the ninth child of the immigrant William Tuttle. He trained as a cooper and lived first in New Haven, moving to Wallingford, Connecticut, about 1685, after his mother's death. He prospered as a Wallingford landholder and town official, serving as gauger and packer from 1708, and in his later years gave land outright to his own sons Timothy and Daniel. Simon Tuttle died 16 April 1719; his widow, Abigail, died three years later, on 11 August 1722.

Elizabeth Tuttle was born about 1609 in England and crossed to New England with her husband, William, in 1635, when she was about twenty-six. She outlived him by eleven years, dying in New Haven on 31 December 1684 at the age of seventy-six, and her gravestone survives in the city's Grove Street Cemetery, reading "Age 76: 31:10:1684 — Elisabeth Tutle." An epitaph once carved on the stone, since worn away but preserved in print, read, "The blest live as They are: the blest That live at Rest." Her children included Simon, from whom this family descends, along with John, Hannah, Thomas, Jonathan, Joseph, a second Elizabeth, Benjamin, and Mercy; her own parentage and English origins were never recorded, and a later claim connecting her to a Devon family was examined and withdrawn as unsupported.
