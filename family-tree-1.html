John Henry Bell (1901/02 AL – 1944/56)

Ahnentafel #8 · his father’s father’s father (3 generations back) · Documented — person and parent-link established

John Henry Bell was born on 4 August 1901 in Barbour County, Alabama — a date his family still remembered in 2009, and one he wrote down himself on a draft card in 1942. He was the eldest son of Thomas Jefferson Bell and Alice May Bradley, farmers near Louisville, and he appears in their household in 1910 and 1920 simply as “Henry.”

By 1925 he had left for Birmingham, and a Barbour County court recorded him there — “Henry Bell, Birmingham” — when his grandfather’s estate was divided seven ways among Tom’s children. Two years later, on Friday the thirteenth of May 1927, he married Margaret Beatrice Eaken. Both Birmingham papers thought a couple marrying on such a date was worth a humorous column. Their first son arrived six months later.

He drove a truck for a dry cleaner, then painted houses — in Birmingham, and in Atlanta through the middle thirties. There were six children. In June 1940 he was convicted in Jefferson County of desertion and non-support, and served four months of a twelve-month sentence; the prison ledger records him at 163 pounds, physical condition good, teeth bad.

He was released that October, and about a year later he left for good. On 23 February 1942 he registered for the draft in Cincinnati — five foot nine, 160 pounds, blue eyes, brown hair, a knot on his left leg near the hip — and gave, as the person who would always know his address, his mother in Dothan. It is the last document he is known to have signed.

After that he vanishes. His family moved north to Wheaton, Illinois, where his sixteen-year-old son was killed in a car crash in January 1944; the certificate names the father simply as “Henry Bell.” By the time his mother died in December 1956 her obituary listed six surviving children, and he was not among them. He lies in neither family cemetery. The likeliest trace of him is a Henry Bell, fifty-one, white, born in Alabama and recorded as “separated,” lodging in a home for the homeless outside Cincinnati in April 1950 — the only man of that description in the entire county.

Margaret Beatrice Eaken (1908 Sumter AL – 1983 Fulton GA)

Ahnentafel #9 · his father’s father’s mother (3 generations back) · Documented — person and parent-link established

Margaret Beatrice Eaken was born on 10 July 1908 at Cuba, in Sumter County, Alabama — her mother's home county, where her father would later be buried. She was eighteen when she married John Henry Bell on Friday the thirteenth of May 1927, and their first son was born that November.

She had six children by him. He left about 1942, and by January 1944 she had taken the family north to Wheaton, Illinois, where her eldest son was killed in a car crash at sixteen. She remarried in 1948 — Claude B. Smith — and the 1950 census finds her at Lineville, Clay County, Alabama, with her four surviving Bell children in the household.

She spent her later years in Georgia, at Clarkston, and retired from Rich's department store in Atlanta. She died on 16 January 1983, aged seventy-four, and was brought back to Birmingham for burial at Forest Hill, where her infant daughter Marion had been buried half a century before. Her obituary ran in both cities and listed her husband, her four children, her mother and her two brothers. It did not mention her first husband.

For a long time the family believed she died in 1985 — but 1985 was the year her own mother died, and the two had quietly become confused.

Helen May Knott (~1905 Indiana – 1977 Evanston IL)

Ahnentafel #13 · his mother’s father’s mother (3 generations back) · Documented — person and parent-link established

Helen May Knott was born on 12 June 1905 in Indiana and died on 24 April 1977 at Evanston Hospital, Illinois, at the age of seventy-one. Her parents are named on her 1923 marriage application to Gifford Tuttle, and again decades later in her son Robert Dale's obituary. Her death notice named her surviving sons, Robert D. of Evanston and Richard C. of Cincinnati, and her sister Mildred Cooper of Cedar Rapids. Three photographs survive of her childhood in Terre Haute about 1911 to 1913: one on the lawn beside her big sister Mildred, seven years her elder, which their mother sent as a postcard inscribed “My too sweet dauters, Mildred and Helen”; and two of Helen with her friend Grace, both girls in wide straw hats. The friend was almost certainly Grace Estella Schomer, born six months after Helen in December 1905, the daughter of the man who ran Terre Haute's dancing school — a child performer the local paper once called “the miniature Pavlowa.” She lived to be ninety-seven. A hand-tinted studio portrait shows her in her late thirties, and another photograph catches her with Gifford in the early 1970s, near their fiftieth anniversary.
