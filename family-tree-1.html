Robert Lee Bell Jr. (b. 1961 Birmingham)

Ahnentafel #2 · his father (1 generation back) · Documented — person and parent-link established

Robert Lee Bell Jr. was born in 1961 in Birmingham, Alabama, the first son of Robert Lee Bell Sr. and Willie Joyce Stephens. The Birmingham News printed his birth that week under the names “Mr. and Mrs. Robert Lee Bell (Willie Joyce Stephens).” His father was four years into a nine-year enlistment in the Air Force, so his first years were lived around postings — his younger brother was born at Fort Worth, Texas, in 1964 — and the earliest photograph of him is from the weeks after his birth, in the arms of his grandmother Margaret Beatrice Eaken.

His parents separated when he was about eleven. His mother married again in 1980 and died in 1982, at forty, when he was barely twenty-one. A school portrait survives from the late 1970s, and another photograph from the late 1980s shows him in a suit at a pharmacy career fair, at the booth of the SuperX drugstore chain.

In 1992 he married Cynthia Katharine Tuttle, and they settled in Cincinnati, where their son Alexander was born in 1997. A photograph from a spring afternoon about 2000 or 2001 shows him in a yard with his own father, his wife and his two sons — three generations of Bells together, not long before his father died. He has since retired, and lives in Palm Springs, California.

Cynthia Katharine Tuttle (b. 1959 Evanston IL)

Ahnentafel #3 · his mother (1 generation back) · Documented — person and parent-link established

Cynthia Katharine Tuttle was born in 1959 in Evanston, Illinois, the daughter of Richard Clark Tuttle and Judith Katharine Frost. Her parents had met at Evanston, where her mother took a degree at Northwestern, and when they were expecting her they bought a house in Deerfield, a northern suburb of Chicago. She was the first of four children.

In 1971, when she was eleven, the family moved to Cincinnati: her father had taken a job with H. S. Crocker, a label printer, after his Chicago firm closed. She grew up there, while her mother taught at the Indian Hill schools and painted portraits. Her grandmother Velma Roush Frost had died when she was not yet five, and she kept what the family remembered of her.

In 1992 she married Robert Lee Bell Jr., and their son Alexander was born in Cincinnati in 1997. She lives in Cincinnati still, and is retired.

Robert Lee Bell Sr. (1935 Atlanta – 2001 Chattanooga)

Ahnentafel #4 · his father’s father (2 generations back) · Documented — person and parent-link established

Robert Lee Bell Sr. was born on 5 November 1935, the fourth child of John Henry Bell, a house painter, and Margaret Beatrice Eaken. His family was moving between Birmingham and Atlanta in the very year he was born, and the records never agreed about which city it had been: the Social Security file says Birmingham, his own obituary says Atlanta, and the 1940 census — which found him at four years old and gave the household’s 1935 address as Atlanta — says Georgia. He may not have been certain himself.

His father walked out about 1942, when he was six, and never came back. The family went north to Wheaton, Illinois, where in January 1944 his eldest brother Thomas was killed in a car crash at sixteen; the death notice in the Birmingham papers lists “Bobby, 8” among the brothers left.

He enlisted in the United States Air Force on 4 February 1957, at twenty-one, and served nine years and ten days, to 14 February 1966, as an Airman Third Class. The service note on his veteran’s record names two wars — Korea and Vietnam — and the marker later cut for him credits him with service in both branches, as A3 in the Air Force and as a staff sergeant in the Army. Not one word of any of it appears in the family’s own papers; it was recoverable only because he was commemorated in a national cemetery.

He married Willie Joyce Stephens at Fort Payne in August 1960, three and a half years into his enlistment. Their first son was born at Birmingham in 1961 — the Birmingham News printed the birth, giving the parents as “Mr. and Mrs. Robert Lee Bell (Willie Joyce Stephens)” and the second at Fort Worth, Texas, in 1964 — Carswell Air Force Base is at Fort Worth, which is why an Alabama family was in Texas at all. So the first six years of that marriage were service years, lived at postings, with a husband twice overseas. It ended about 1972–73, and he married again afterwards. In January 1983 his mother’s obituary placed him at Mobile, Alabama, named among her four surviving children.

The later years were not quiet ones. On 20 November 1989 police in Blount County, Tennessee, searched a pair of massage businesses called Sue Lynn’s Health Studio and the 129 Health Studio, a mobile home and a dumpster, and he was charged with running them as brothels. In March 1990 he was convicted on five counts of promoting prostitution, for offences between 1987 and 1989, and sentenced to five consecutive two-year terms — ten years — and a $3,000 fine. The appeals court, affirming every part of it in August 1991, described an operation reaching at least seven establishments across several states, from Sevierville and Chattanooga to Fort Lauderdale, Florida; it noted an earlier guilty plea to possessing cocaine, that he had declined to give the probation officers an employment history, and it upheld the consecutive terms on the finding that he had made crime a major source of his livelihood. The Tennessee prison record for Robert Bell, born 5 November 1935 — six foot two, 170 pounds, blue eyes, grey hair — shows the sentence running out on 29 April 1999. It is the likeliest reason he ended his life in Chattanooga.

He died at Chattanooga on 14 February 2001, aged sixty-five — thirty-five years to the day after his discharge. Four photographs survive from his last years: beside a Tennessee lake in winter, deep-sea fishing in a straw hat, a plain portrait in a blue polo shirt, and one taken in a spring yard about 2000 or 2001, where he stands with his son, his daughter-in-law and his two grandsons. His name is cut on a white marble marker in the memorial section at Chattanooga National Cemetery, Section MA, site 36, beneath a cross and the words IN MEMORY OF.

Willie Joyce Stephens (1942 York – 1982 Jefferson Co.)

Ahnentafel #5 · his father’s mother (2 generations back) · Documented — person and parent-link established

Willie Joyce Stephens was born on 19 March 1942 at York, her father’s town in Sumter County, Alabama, the daughter of Jess Steve Willard Stephens, a mechanic who went by “Willard” and “J. W.,” and Grace Estelle Truett of Coffeeville in Clarke County. Her parents’ marriage did not last, and she was raised in Birmingham.

She went to Ramsay High School and transferred to Phillips in her third term, graduating there in 1960. Her senior year was a busy one: a Senior Y-Teen and its treasurer, a member of the Student Representative Council, a Red Cross representative and a Marshal. Her photograph in the Phillips yearbook is the earliest likeness of her there is, and it was taken only months before she married.

On 26 August 1960, aged eighteen, she married Robert Lee Bell at Fort Payne, in DeKalb County. Her first son was born in 1961, and a second followed in 1964 at Fort Worth. Her husband left the family about 1972 or 1973.

On 24 May 1980 she married again, to John Duncan Lammon, in the same town where she had married the first time. It was a home wedding, not a church one — a hired brass arch dressed with pink and blue flowers set up in somebody’s living room, white tapers in candelabra on either side, the bride in a full-length ivory gown with long sheer sleeves and the groom in a pale blue three-piece suit. She was thirty-eight.

She moved south with him to Jackson, in Clarke County, and there she kept her own clothing shop. She died on Tuesday 27 July 1982, aged forty; what took her is not known. Her husband survived her, and so did her mother Grace and her stepfather Charles L. Roberts of Birmingham, and her two sons and a daughter. The funeral was at Latham Funeral Chapel the next day. She was buried at Mount Gilead Cemetery in Clarke County — Lammon ground, where her second husband and the Lammon generation before him also lie. Her stone calls her simply JOYCE STEPHENS LAMMON, and under the dates it carries the whole of Micah 6:8: “He hath shewed thee, O man, what is good: and what doth the Lord require of thee, but to do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy God.” In the lower corner, in smaller letters, someone had two more words cut: AN ANGEL.

Besides the yearbook portrait, three photographs of her survive, all from her last two years: her wedding day; a restaurant above the water with her husband’s arm around her shoulders; and one of her alone in a panelled room hung with framed stamp collections.

Richard Clark Tuttle (1929 Terre Haute – 2015 Cincinnati)

Ahnentafel #6 · his mother’s father (2 generations back) · Documented — person and parent-link established

Richard Clark Tuttle — Dick, all his life — was born at Terre Haute, Indiana, on 29 or 30 July 1929, the second son of Gifford Herman Tuttle and Helen May Knott, who were then living at 2266 Warren Street. His people had been in and around Terre Haute since the 1850s.

He grew up with an older brother, Robert Dale; a photograph from about 1938 has the two of them on a patch of spring grass, Dick at nine in a sweater and short trousers. When he was ten his grandmother Martha was living in the house as well — a woman he spent years under one roof with, and about whom almost nothing came down in the family afterwards.

About 1944 or 1945, when he was fifteen, his father moved the family north to Evanston, Illinois, and this branch of the Tuttles left Indiana after nearly a century. He graduated from Evanston Township High School in the class of 1947 — his senior portrait is on the class pages of The Evanstonian of ’47, and the family still has his own copy of the book with his name printed in the corner — and went on to take classes at Northwestern.

He enlisted in the United States Army and was certified at Fort Lee, Virginia, on 3 August 1951 as a quartermaster supply-records specialist, then served in occupied Germany; a photograph kept in the family shows him in uniform beside the sign for the Headquarters, Heilbronn Sub Post. He was still there at the end of 1952, sending his congratulations home to Terre Haute for his grandparents’ golden wedding.

Back in Evanston he met Judith Frost, who had come up from Marion, Indiana, to take a degree at Northwestern; they were introduced, the family says, through a friend. They married in the middle fifties, and in 1959, expecting their first child, bought a house on Dartmouth Avenue in Deerfield, Illinois. He worked for a Chicago label printer until the owner of the firm killed himself with carbon monoxide, and in 1971 he moved the family to Cincinnati to take a job with H. S. Crocker Co., another label company. He lived out his life there, and died at Cincinnati in 2015, two years after Judith.

Judith Katharine Frost (1934 Parkersburg – 2013 Cincinnati)

Ahnentafel #7 · his mother’s mother (2 generations back) · Documented — person and parent-link established

Judith Katharine Frost was born on 5 July 1934 at Parkersburg, West Virginia, the only child of Alan Chalmers Frost, who managed dime stores, and Velma Gladys Roush, a Mason County girl who had run away from home at sixteen. At two and a half, about 1936, she sat for a studio portrait, grinning in a knitted sweater-dress, ankle socks and white lace-up shoes.

Her father’s work moved them. By 1940 the family was renting on Stocking Road in Mansfield, Ohio, for thirty dollars a month; by 1950 they were at Marion, Indiana, where he ran the S. S. Kresge store seventy hours a week. In March 1947, when she was twelve, her mother took her to Akron to stay with her aunt Florence, and the two of them went on to Mason, West Virginia, to the bedside of her grandmother Bertha, who lay ill at home; another aunt came up from Georgia to join them.

She graduated from Marion High School in 1952 — Latin Club, Booster Club, Y-Teens, Student Council, and co-chairman of the Mother-Daughter Christmas Tea — and went on to college, to Earlham by the family’s account and then to Northwestern. It was at Evanston that she met Richard Clark Tuttle. Their engagement was announced in April 1957, her engagement portrait ran in the Marion paper in May, they took out a licence in Cook County on 26 June, and they were married at three o’clock on Saturday 29 June 1957 in St. Luke’s Chapel, Evanston, by the Rev. St. John Brown. A photograph of her at a college formal in the early 1950s — a dark floor-length gown, a young man beside her in a dark suit with a white boutonnière — belongs to the few years before that. Their first home was at 536 Michigan Avenue, Evanston.

They bought a house on Dartmouth Avenue in Deerfield, Illinois in 1959, and moved to Cincinnati in 1971 when Richard took a job with the label printer H. S. Crocker. There were four children. She taught at the Indian Hill Schools, where she was remembered as “a positive early influence for hundreds of small children,” and she was a portrait painter and a maker of very fine dollhouse miniatures. To her grandchildren she was Mimi.

She died at Cincinnati on 31 October 2013, after fifty-six years of marriage. Her funeral was held on 5 November at St. Barnabas Episcopal Church on Montgomery Road.

John Henry Bell (1901/02 AL – 1944/56)

Ahnentafel #8 · his father’s father’s father (3 generations back) · Documented — person and parent-link established

John Henry Bell was born on 4 August 1901 in Barbour County, Alabama — a date his family still remembered in 2009, and one he wrote down himself on a draft card in 1942. He was the eldest son of Thomas Jefferson Bell and Alice May Bradley, farmers near Louisville, and he appears in their household in 1910 and 1920 simply as “Henry.”

By 1925 he had left for Birmingham, and a Barbour County court recorded him there — “Henry Bell, Birmingham” — when his grandfather’s estate was divided seven ways among Tom’s children. Two years later, on Friday the thirteenth of May 1927, he married Margaret Beatrice Eaken. Both Birmingham papers thought a couple marrying on such a date was worth a humorous column. Their first son arrived six months later, and he named the boy Thomas J., for his own father.

He drove a truck for a dry cleaner, then painted houses — in Birmingham, and in Atlanta through the middle thirties. There were six children. The second, a daughter named Marion, was born and lost in 1932 and buried at Forest Hill in Birmingham — his wife’s ground, and the one family burying place he would not be laid in either. In June 1940 he was convicted in Jefferson County of desertion and non-support, and served four months of a twelve-month sentence; the prison ledger records him at 163 pounds, physical condition good, teeth bad.

He was released on 17 October 1940 by order of Judge Emmett Perry, and about a year later he left for good. On 23 February 1942 he registered for the draft in Cincinnati — five foot nine, 160 pounds, blue eyes, brown hair, a knot on his left leg near the hip — and gave, as the person who would always know his address, his mother in Dothan. It is the last document he is known to have signed.

After that he vanishes. His family moved north to Wheaton, Illinois, where his sixteen-year-old son was killed in a car crash in January 1944; the certificate names the father simply as “Henry Bell.” By the time his mother died in December 1956 her obituary listed six surviving children, and he was not among them. He lies in neither family cemetery — not at Forest Hill with his wife and his baby daughter, and not at Bethlehem with his parents. The likeliest trace of him is a Henry Bell, fifty-one, white, born in Alabama and recorded as “separated,” lodging in a home for the homeless outside Cincinnati in April 1950 — the only man of that description in the entire county.
