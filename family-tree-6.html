Samuel Allis (24 Feb 1647 Braintree – 9 Mar 1691 Hatfield; carpenter and builder)

Ahnentafel #3076 · his mother’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s mother’s father’s father (11 generations back) · Documented — person and parent-link established

Samuel Allis was born in Braintree, Massachusetts, on 24 February 1647, the son of Lieutenant William Allis. He worked as a carpenter and builder in Hatfield, where the town granted him a house lot as a young man of about twenty-two, and he lived on the east side of Hatfield street. He married Alice about 1675-6, and together they had seven children, among them William, born 19 October 1680, from whom this family line descends. Samuel Allis died in Hatfield on 9 March 1691, leaving his widow to raise their children alone; two of those children were later caught up in the 1704 raid on Deerfield that killed their mother — one son killed alongside her, and a daughter carried captive to Canada who survived and came home.

Mr. John Catlin (b. about 1643 – killed defending his house at Deerfield, 29 Feb 1704)

Ahnentafel #3082 · his mother’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s mother’s father’s mother’s father (11 generations back) · Documented — person and parent-link established

John Catlin was born about 1643, the son of a Catlin family of Wethersfield. He lived successively in Wethersfield, Branford, and Newark, New Jersey — where he served as a schoolteacher in 1676, as the town's attorney, and as a selectman for five years — before moving to Hartford and then, about 1683, to Deerfield, Massachusetts, where he was given the honorific title "Mr." and took a leading part in town affairs. On 29 February 1704, during the French and Indian raid on Deerfield, John Catlin and his son Jonathan were killed defending their house, which was then burned; another son was killed in the fighting that followed in the meadow, and a third son, also John, was carried captive to Canada, later returning to rebuild on the same ground and becoming known locally as "Master Catlin." Two of his daughters, Elizabeth and Mary, died on the forced march to Canada after the raid; a third daughter, Sarah, had already married and left the household, which is why this line of the family survived at all.

Mary Baldwin (bap. 23 June 1644; m. 23 Sept 1662 – survived the raid of 29 Feb 1704)

Ahnentafel #3083 · his mother’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s mother’s father’s mother’s mother (11 generations back) · Documented — person and parent-link established

Mary Baldwin was baptized 23 June 1644, the daughter of Joseph Baldwin of Milford, Connecticut, and his first wife, Hannah. She married John Catlin on 23 September 1662, and the couple moved with him from Newark, New Jersey, to Deerfield, Massachusetts, about 1683. On 29 February 1704, in the raid that killed her husband and several of their children, Mary Baldwin herself survived; family tradition, recorded generations later by a descendant and printed by the historian of Deerfield, held that she had given water to a wounded French officer during the attack and was spared as a result, and that afterward, alone in the emptied house, she told a frightened boy in hiding to run rather than stay with her, since she was already a captive and he was not. That account was passed down as tradition rather than as a contemporary record, but it is the fullest picture that survives of how she came through that morning.

William Davis lived in Roxbury, Massachusetts, where a 1649 church record names his sons John, Samuel, and Joseph among the children of "William Davis whose wife was admitted" to the congregation. His son Samuel was later dismissed from the Roxbury church to Northampton. William Davis had two wives — Elizabeth, and later Alice Thorp, who died in childbirth — and was, according to one genealogist, probably a brother of a man named Tobias. He died 9 December 1683, at about the age of sixty-six.

Deacon William Peck (b. 1601 London – d. 4 (or 14) Oct 1694 at Lyme, at his son Joseph’s house)

Ahnentafel #12300 · his mother’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s mother’s mother’s father’s father (13 generations back) · Documented — person and parent-link established · Immigrant

William Peck was born in London in 1601 and worked there as a merchant before emigrating with his wife, Elizabeth, and son Jeremiah, probably on the ship Hector, in the company of Governors Eaton and Hopkins and the Reverend John Davenport. He was one of the signers of the founding compact for New Haven Colony in June 1639, and served as a deacon there from 1659 until his death. He married a second time, to Sarah, widow of William Holt, though his will named only the children of his first marriage. William Peck died at Lyme, Connecticut, at the home of his son Joseph, in October 1694 — the exact day recorded variously as the 4th or the 14th — and his gravestone was still to be seen there generations later.
