Lt. William Allis (freeman of Braintree 1640; commanded the Hatfield garrison 1676 – d. Sept 1678)

Ahnentafel #6152 · his mother’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s mother’s father’s father’s father (12 generations back) · Documented — person and parent-link established · Immigrant

William Allis became a freeman at Braintree, Massachusetts, in 1640 and removed to Hatfield about 1661. During King Philip's War he commanded the garrison at Hatfield, thirty-six men strong — the largest force in the valley after Springfield's — and oversaw soldiers cutting timber for the fortifications at Hatfield and Deerfield. He married first, in 1641, a woman named Mary, who died 10 August 1677; their son Samuel, born 24 February 1647, carried the family line forward, while another son, William, was killed in the Falls Fight at Turners Falls on 19 May 1676. Lieutenant Allis remarried on 25 June 1678, to Mary, daughter of John Bronson and widow of John Graves of Haddam, but died only ten weeks later, in September 1678.

Mary (first wife of Lt. William Allis; m. 1641 – d. 10 August 1677)

Ahnentafel #6153 · his mother’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s mother’s father’s father’s mother (12 generations back) · Documented — person and parent-link established

Mary married William Allis in 1641 and bore him several children, among them Samuel, born 1647, from whom this family descends, and a son William, later killed in the Falls Fight of 1676. She died 10 August 1677. Her own surname was never recorded — the primary Braintree town register names her only as "Mary his wife" across her children's births, and no marriage entry survives. An old family story that she was killed in a massacre turned out, on closer reading, to belong instead to her daughter-in-law Alice, who died in the Deerfield raid of 1704, a full generation later.

William Davis (c.1617 – 9 Dec 1683 (æ66), Roxbury, Mass.)

William Davis lived in Roxbury, Massachusetts, where a 1649 church record names his sons John, Samuel, and Joseph among the children of "William Davis whose wife was admitted" to the congregation. His son Samuel was later dismissed from the Roxbury church to Northampton. William Davis had two wives — Elizabeth, and later Alice Thorp, who died in childbirth — and was, according to one genealogist, probably a brother of a man named Tobias. He died 9 December 1683, at about the age of sixty-six.

Isabel married three times. Deacon William Peck (b. 1601 London – d. 4 (or 14) Oct 1694 at Lyme, at his son Joseph’s house)

Ahnentafel #12300 · his mother’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s father’s mother’s mother’s father’s father (13 generations back) · Documented — person and parent-link established · Immigrant

William Peck was born in London in 1601 and worked there as a merchant before emigrating with his wife, Elizabeth, and son Jeremiah, probably on the ship Hector, in the company of Governors Eaton and Hopkins and the Reverend John Davenport. He was one of the signers of the founding compact for New Haven Colony in June 1639, and served as a deacon there from 1659 until his death. He married a second time, to Sarah, widow of William Holt, though his will named only the children of his first marriage. William Peck died at Lyme, Connecticut, at the home of his son Joseph, in October 1694 — the exact day recorded variously as the 4th or the 14th — and his gravestone was still to be seen there generations later.

John Moss was in New Haven by 1639 and later moved to Wallingford, Connecticut, by 1670. He and his wife had eleven children baptized between 1640 and 1655, among them Mary, baptized 11 April 1647, who married John Peck in 1664. One later compiler called him "the ancestor of all who bear the name in these parts."
