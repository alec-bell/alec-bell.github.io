Thomas Jefferson Bell (b. 1875×1878 – d. 30 Aug 1921, near Louisville, Barbour Co., Alabama)

Ahnentafel #16 · his father’s father’s father’s father (4 generations back) · Documented — person and parent-link established

Thomas Jefferson Bell farmed near Louisville in Barbour County, Alabama. He married Alice May Bradley on 13 August 1895, when she was eighteen, and they had nine children — seven still living when the 1920 census found them together, and two already buried at Bethlehem Baptist churchyard.

He died on 30 August 1921 and was laid beside those two children. He left no estate: the county's probate minutes for the year after his death record no administration in his name, and the clerk's index to seventy years of administrators' records contains no Thomas J. Bell at all. There was simply nothing to divide.

So when his children finally received something, it came through his own father's estate instead. In April 1925 a Barbour County court divided one deceased heir's share seven ways among Tom's children, naming each of them — Ellie, Ray, Ruth, William, Ethel, little Juanita, and “Henry Bell, Birmingham.” That decree is the court's own answer to the question of who his children were, and it is the document that finally settled this family's longest-standing puzzle.

Alice May Bradley (8 Oct 1876 – 29 Dec 1956, Montgomery Co., Alabama; m.(2) Mack K. Norton)

Ahnentafel #17 · his father’s father’s father’s mother (4 generations back) · Documented — person and parent-link established

Alice May Bradley was born on 8 October 1876 in Barbour County, Alabama, the daughter of Henry Bradley and Elvira Loveless. Her father died young: in October 1891 a county court appointed a guardian for Alice, then fourteen, and her sister Katie, twelve, noting that the minors had no father living.

She married Thomas Jefferson Bell on 13 August 1895, at eighteen, and had nine children. Widowed in 1921, she married Mack K. Norton — and there is a quiet symmetry in it, because Mack's first wife had been her own sister Katie, who died in 1924. Two Bradley sisters each married a Bell, each was widowed, and the same man married them in turn.

By 1935 she was living with Mack at 118 East North Street in Dothan, and it was that address her son wrote on his draft card in 1942 when the form asked who would always know where he was. Mack died in 1954; his obituary called her children his step-sons and step-daughters, naming them as Bells, which is how the family was finally proved from the outside.

She outlived her husband by thirty-five years and died at Montgomery on 29 December 1956, aged eighty. They brought her home to Bethlehem, in Barbour County, and buried her beside him under the name Bell — not Norton.
